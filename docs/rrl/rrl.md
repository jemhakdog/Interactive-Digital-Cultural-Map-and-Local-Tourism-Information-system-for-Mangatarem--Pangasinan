Methodologies for Digital Cultural Mapping in the
## Philippine Context
The development of an Interactive Digital Cultural Map and Information System for
Mangatarem, Pangasinan, is situated within a growing body of work focused on
leveraging technology for cultural documentation and local development. An
examination of recent literature reveals that digital cultural mapping in the Philippines is
not merely a technical exercise but a structured process grounded in national policy,
institutional frameworks, and participatory methodologies. This foundation provides a
robust blueprint for the proposed project, outlining both established precedents and
critical challenges that must be navigated for successful implementation. The core
methodology emerging from the literature is Participatory Geographic Information
Systems (PGIS), which emphasizes community engagement over top-down data
acquisition, ensuring that the resulting map reflects authentic local values and knowledge
. This approach is particularly salient for a municipality like Mangatarem, where the
goal is not only to document heritage but also to foster a sense of ownership and pride
among its residents.
The conceptualization of cultural mapping in the Philippines is strongly supported by
national-level mandates and institutional actors. The National Commission for Culture
and the Arts (NCCA) plays a pivotal role in guiding and supporting cultural mapping
projects across the country, working in collaboration with Local Government Units
(LGUs) . The legal framework for this work is often rooted in laws that recognize
the importance of preserving the nation's cultural assets, providing a clear justification for
municipal-level initiatives . LGUs are recognized as the primary implementers of these
programs at the grassroots level, tasked with identifying, documenting, and promoting
their unique cultural landscapes . The Department of Tourism (DOT) further reinforces
this by acknowledging the crucial role of cultural mapping in developing sustainable
tourism products and destinations . This alignment between national policy,
institutional support, and sectoral strategy creates a fertile environment for a project like

Several recent studies have provided concrete examples of how cultural mapping has
been successfully implemented at the municipal and provincial levels, offering direct
precedents for the Mangatarem project. A case study from San Pablo City detailed the
process of creating a cultural map of its built heritages, aiming for greater accuracy and

comprehensive culture map that chronicled the events shaping the island province's
identity . These examples demonstrate that the process involves more than just
cartography; it requires historical research, community consultation, and strategic
planning to create a resource that serves both administrative and promotional purposes.
The Municipality of Narra, for instance, utilized its cultural map to support the functions
of its Municipal Tourism Development and Promotions Office (MTDPO), showing a direct
link between the mapping initiative and economic development goals . The experience
of the Ifugao Rice Terraces, where a heritage cluster was integrated into the
municipality's Comprehensive Land-use Plan (CLUP), highlights the potential for cultural
mapping to influence long-term land use and conservation strategies . For
Mangatarem, these precedents suggest that the digital map can serve multiple functions:
as a tool for heritage conservation, a guide for sustainable tourism planning, and a
reference for educational and community-building activities. The literature suggests that
the scope of such a map should encompass not only tangible assets like historical
buildings and archaeological sites but also intangible aspects like traditions and
community practices, which are often documented through cultural mapping projects .
The dominant methodological approach identified in the literature is Participatory GIS
(PGIS). PGIS is defined as an approach that combines traditional GIS technologies with
participatory learning and action methods, empowering communities to become active
participants in the mapping process . This is a significant departure from conventional
top-down mapping, which relies solely on expert-driven data collection. The literature
consistently emphasizes that involving local stakeholders—including officials, elders,
artists, and residents—is critical to the success and legitimacy of any cultural mapping
project . By partnering with the community, researchers and planners can tap into
local knowledge that may not be recorded in official archives, leading to a more nuanced
and accurate representation of the cultural landscape . Studies have demonstrated the
application of PGIS for a wide range of purposes relevant to Mangatarem, including the
identification of tangible and intangible cultural heritage, community-based disaster
management, and sustainable land use planning . For example, a study on

Despite the strong theoretical and policy support for cultural mapping, the literature also
reveals a series of significant challenges that have impeded its widespread and effective
implementation in the Philippines. One major barrier identified is the lack of formal
agreements, such as Memoranda of Agreements (MOAs), between LGUs and other
implementing bodies or academic institutions, which can lead to confusion and stalled
progress . Furthermore, there are documented difficulties in obtaining necessary
permits for fieldwork and data collection, which can delay projects and limit the scope of
investigation . Beyond logistical hurdles, there are deeper systemic issues. Research
has pointed out a dissonance between national heritage policies and their actual
execution at the local level, often due to conflicting interests, lack of resources, and
insufficient capacity within LGUs . The diversity of GIS capabilities across different
LGUs in the Philippines indicates that some units may lack the technical expertise or
infrastructure to effectively undertake a complex mapping project . The failure of
inter-organisational cooperation between various local government stakeholders during
system development has also been cited as a significant difficulty . These challenges
are not merely obstacles but represent critical areas where the Mangatarem project can
innovate. By proactively establishing formal partnerships, navigating bureaucratic
processes early on, and designing the system to be accessible even to users with limited
technical skills, the project can overcome these common pitfalls and serve as a model for
other municipalities. The literature thus provides a dual-purpose guide: it showcases
successful models to emulate while also flagging potential failures to avoid.

## Heritage Preservation
The effectiveness of an Interactive Digital Cultural Map for Mangatarem hinges critically
on its ability to engage the local community and accurately represent the full spectrum of
its cultural assets, particularly its intangible cultural heritage (ICH). The reviewed
literature overwhelmingly supports the adoption of a Community-Based Information
System (CBIS) approach, where the community is not merely a passive subject of data

The concept of a CBIS is central to the success of any digital heritage project intended for
public use. Unlike systems developed solely by external experts, a CBIS is designed and
maintained in close collaboration with the community it serves . The ATIPAN Project,
a digital health strategy aimed at marginalized communities in Western Visayas,
Philippines, exemplifies this principle by being a community-based digital health strategy
. In the context of Mangatarem, this means involving local historians, indigenous
knowledge holders, artisans, youth leaders, and ordinary residents in every stage of the
project, from defining what constitutes cultural heritage to designing the user interface
and populating the database. This participatory approach ensures that the system reflects
the authentic voice and priorities of the people of Mangatarem, increasing its credibility
and encouraging sustained use. The literature underscores that such community
engagement is not just beneficial but essential for building trust and ensuring that the
system addresses genuine local needs . The project can draw inspiration from a case
study in Del Carmen, a rural municipality that successfully implemented a social
innovation program driven by local empowerment . For Mangatarem, this translates to
a project that empowers residents to tell their own stories, share their skills, and take
ownership of their digital representation. This bottom-up approach is vital for
overcoming the common challenge of low social participation in heritage conservation,
which is noted as a problem in many developing countries .

monuments and artifacts . ICH is inherently ephemeral, transmitted orally and
experientially, making its preservation a multifaceted challenge . The literature

Technological Frameworks for Interactive Heritage
## Platforms
The selection of an appropriate technological framework is a cornerstone of the
Interactive Digital Cultural Map and Information System for Mangatarem. The literature
from 2020-2025 clearly indicates a convergence towards web-based Geographic
Information Systems (WebGIS) as the foundational technology for creating accessible,
scalable, and interactive cultural heritage platforms . This approach makes
spatial data available through standard web browsers, eliminating the need for
specialized and expensive software installations, thereby democratizing access for a wider
audience of residents, students, and tourists. Beyond the core WebGIS architecture, the
literature points to several key features and implementation strategies that can enhance
user engagement and the overall effectiveness of the system. These include incorporating
interactive storytelling elements, leveraging immersive technologies like Virtual Reality
(VR) for more engaging experiences, and adopting a modular design that allows for
future expansion and integration with other digital services. The successful
implementation of such a system, however, depends not only on cutting-edge technology
but also on adhering to best practices in software engineering, such as ensuring
maintainability and planning for long-term sustainability.
WebGIS has emerged as the de facto standard for digital cultural mapping projects
globally and is increasingly adopted in the Philippines . Its primary advantage is
accessibility; a WebGIS platform can be accessed from any device with a web browser
and an internet connection, including desktop computers, tablets, and smartphones .
This broadens the potential user base significantly compared to standalone desktop GIS
applications. Recent studies have demonstrated the versatility of WebGIS for a variety of
cultural heritage applications. For example, researchers have developed WebGIS
platforms to visualize historical activities based on archival photographs, allowing users

To be truly "interactive," a digital map must transcend simple point-and-click
functionality and offer engaging, multi-layered experiences. The literature suggests
several strategies for achieving this. One powerful technique is the use of interactive
timelines and multimedia galleries. By overlaying historical photographs onto current
satellite imagery or maps, users can gain a vivid sense of the area's historical evolution
. The Gusu Prosperous Map, a historical visualization of Qing Dynasty Suzhou,
demonstrates the power of data-rich visualizations in conveying cultural landscapes .
For Mangatarem, this could involve creating interactive layers that show the town's
layout in different decades or highlight the history of specific buildings. Incorporating
audio and video content is another way to bring intangible heritage to life . The system

demonstrations of craft-making processes. This aligns with the growing trend of using
immersive technologies to create engaging cultural experiences . While full-fledged
VR might be a long-term goal, simpler forms of interactive storytelling, such as narrative-
driven tours or augmented reality (AR) features accessible via smartphone, can make the
map more compelling. The use of projection mapping in museums to connect tangible
and intangible heritage offers another model for how to present complex cultural
narratives in an engaging format . The key is to move away from a purely
informational interface and towards an experience-oriented one that encourages
exploration and discovery.

The ISO/IEC 25010 standard, also known as the Systems and software engineering —
Product Quality model, provides a structured framework for evaluating software product
quality . It defines eight primary quality characteristics, each with sub-
characteristics, offering a holistic view of a system's performance . The relevance of
this standard to the Mangatarem project is explicitly mentioned in multiple studies
focused on tourism information systems, e-learning platforms, and mobile applications
. The eight main characteristics are: Functional Suitability, Performance
Efficiency, Compatibility, Usability, Reliability, Security, Maintainability, and Portability
. For a public-facing cultural map, several of these are particularly critical. Functional
Suitability determines whether the system correctly performs its intended functions, such
as accurately displaying map data and providing correct information about heritage sites
. Performance Efficiency relates to the system's responsiveness and resource usage,
which is crucial for a positive user experience, especially if the map contains many high-
resolution images or videos . Compatibility ensures the system works across different
devices and browsers, maximizing its reach . Most importantly, Usability measures
how easy the system is for users to learn, operate, and perceive . This characteristic is
arguably the most important for a public-facing system, as poor usability is a primary
reason for user abandonment .
Usability, as defined by ISO/IEC 25010, encompasses several key attributes that are
highly relevant to the design of the Mangatarem map. These include Appropriateness,
which corresponds to suitability for the task; Learnability, the ease with which users can
begin effective interaction; Operability, the extent to which users can operate and
control the system effectively; and Attractiveness, the aesthetic appeal and ergonomics
of the user interface . The literature emphasizes the importance of applying these
principles in practice. For example, a study on evaluating the implementation

addressing both usability and accessibility, the Mangatarem project can create an

Implementation Challenges and Sustainability in Rural
## Municipalities
While the technological and methodological foundations for an Interactive Digital
Cultural Map are well-established in the literature, the path from concept to a
sustainable, functioning system is laden with significant implementation challenges,
especially within the specific context of a rural Philippine municipality like Mangatarem.
The literature from 2020-2025 highlights a confluence of factors—including digital
divides, organizational barriers, and persistent funding gaps—that can derail even the
most well-designed projects. Addressing these challenges proactively is not optional; it is
a prerequisite for success. The project's implementation plan must be as robust as its
technological design, incorporating strategies for building local capacity, fostering inter-
organizational collaboration, and securing long-term financial viability. Success in this
domain requires a socio-technical approach that recognizes that technology alone cannot
solve complex social and administrative problems.
One of the most pervasive challenges identified is the existence of digital inequalities,
which can create a significant "usability gap" and limit community participation . In
the Philippines, disparities in internet access, device ownership, and digital literacy are
particularly pronounced in rural areas . A study on digital literacy gaps in a rural high
school in the Philippines revealed the specific challenges faced by students in accessing
and effectively using digital tools . For the Mangatarem project, this has profound
implications. It suggests that the system cannot rely solely on a high-bandwidth, feature-
rich web interface. A tiered approach may be necessary, offering a lightweight version of
the map optimized for mobile phones and low-bandwidth connections. Furthermore, the
project must include a substantial community engagement and training component.
Workshops and training sessions should be conducted to teach residents how to use the
platform, contribute content, and understand the cultural information available. Without
this investment in digital inclusion, the project risks becoming a tool that only benefits
the tech-savvy minority, failing to achieve its goal of being a community-wide resource.

The literature collectively argues that a successful project must be a socio-technical

approach, rigorous quality assurance, and a pragmatic plan for long-term sustainability.
The literature firmly establishes that the methodology for this project is well-trodden,
primarily through the adoption of Participatory Geographic Information Systems (PGIS)

initiatives in San Pablo City and Guimaras , and international best practices . The

the Mangatarem project can foster a sense of ownership and pride, transforming the map
from a static inventory into a living, evolving cultural repository. Furthermore, the
literature provides a strong mandate for this focus, with national bodies like the NCCA
and DOT actively promoting cultural mapping as a tool for both heritage conservation
and tourism development .
